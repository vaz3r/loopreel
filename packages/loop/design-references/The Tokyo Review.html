The Room Between

Abstract

An inquiry into thresholds, light, and the space we choose not to fill. This essay proposes reading the interior through its permissions: where arrival can become a pause, where light makes work possible, and where furniture leaves room for an unforeseen use. Not an argument for owning less, but for asking more of what remains.

PEER REVIEWED

Read the Relationships

THREE WAYS TO READ A ROOM
01

NOTICE THE INTERVAL

Read the threshold as a potential place, not merely a boundary. Ask whether arrival allows someone to put down a bag, negotiate another person's passage, or pause before entering—and whose needs the available depth accommodates.

02

FOLLOW THE LIGHT

Begin with the occupied surface rather than the luminous photograph. Trace light to the page, the table, and the moving body; document the conditions before treating brightness as evidence that a room supports its intended work.

03

QUESTION THE OBJECT

Before removing a cabinet, account for everything it makes possible. Test whether consolidating storage preserves access and necessary belongings while releasing usable floor area. Subtraction earns its place only when essential functions survive it.
